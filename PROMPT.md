# PROMPT.md — Angry Judge: The Short Rant Contest

## The bounty prompt (from the kit's RULES.txt)

> Write a short, funny rant about an everyday annoyance and let a furious
> judge decide which one lands hardest.
>
> Entrants write one short text: a funny rant about a small everyday
> annoyance such as a broken printer, a slow bus or a Monday morning. A
> judge model with a famously short temper reads the rants two at a time
> and picks the one that lands hardest, and the best rant takes the reward.
> Entries are capped at 400 characters, so the punch has to come fast.
>
> The judge weighs, most important first: (1) a funny rant about an
> everyday annoyance, not a general essay or a list of complaints,
> (2) whether it is actually funny, (3) a specific, concrete annoyance
> rather than a vague mood, (4) sharp, economical wording, (5) freshness —
> an angle on the annoyance you have not heard a hundred times before.

Gate: `/output/entry.txt` must be UTF-8 text of 1 to 400 characters.

## Our entry (288 characters)

The self-checkout screamed "UNEXPECTED ITEM IN THE BAGGING AREA" at one banana. One banana! It has scanned a thousand bananas, it knows what a banana is — but today it chose violence, called a human, and made me wait while a teenager pressed a button like a priest absolving my groceries.

## Why this rant

- Specific and concrete (criterion 3): one banana, one machine, one
  teenager — not a vague mood about technology.
- Fresh (criterion 5): the self-checkout banana is a newer, less
  over-mined annoyance than the printer / slow bus / Monday defaults
  most entries will reach for.
- The anger feels shared, not performed (criterion 2): the turn "it has
  scanned a thousand bananas, it knows what a banana is" is the exact
  thought every shopper has had; "chose violence" and the priest image
  close the rant on a punchline instead of trailing into a complaint list.
- Economical (criterion 4): three sentences, no throat-clearing.
